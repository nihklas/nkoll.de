# Intro

I don't remember how exactly but I got into FPV Quadcopter flying. Those are
those fancy videos you see online where people fly these drones with
first-person googles through crazy abandoned places and make incredible tricks.
You know, stuff like [this](https://youtu.be/08cDNbkoIuk?si=V3wxPvPWO9u3F2Lz). I
always found those interesting, but I never go really into it besides a small
toy quad as a kid, that didn't live very long. Now, I really fell deep into the
rabbit hole and set myself a goal to try out the hobby for real.

# My first days

I don't have a lot of money. FPV is an expensive hobby. This will definitely be
a problem in the future, but even to start it is difficult without a small to
medium large investment upfront. The absolute cheapest you can start is a free
simulator you can find somewhere on steam. I read a few reviews and guides on
which Simulator to choose and I went with a sim called "Liftoff", which costs
about 20 Euro on steam. I am very happy with it, it had a small tutorial/intro
where you get introduced into the controls and the basics of flying. After that
you can choose to do races, freestyle or anything you want. It is basically a
sandbox game for FPV drones. The maps also range from absolutely empty, boring
farm field to a very detailed oil-refinery with a trainstation where you can
really explore a lot of small gaps and get very creative with your flight.

To fly even in the sim, you need some kind of controller. Best case, you buy
yourself a radio controller you will use for your real drone later as well but I
started with a PS5 controller because I didn't want to buy just the cheapest
possible controller. Instead I safe up just a little longer to buy a good budget
controller and in the mean time have to use a gamepad. This is actually harder
than expected, because a game controller is quite a bit different than the radio
controllers used for drones or other radio controlled models. The sticks are
shorter, the throttle jumps back into the middle and the form factor doesn't
lend itself very well to a gripping style called "pinching". This makes it hard
to use a PS5 controller, but not totally unusable.

I am doing A LOT of research right now. What controller should I get? What drone
should I get? What goggles? What else do you need to fly? Batteries for the
drone? Which ones? 1S, 2S, LiPo, LiIon, what are those mystical words I don't
fully understand? What controller protocol? FrSky, ELRS? What video
transmission? Analog or digital? DJI, Walksnail? The goggles have to match the
drones video transmission? Analog has less latency but worse video quality? Is
that a problem? OH MY GOD, so many questions and with each one answered I have
at least 3 more. There is just SO MUCH to know about this hobby. But this is
exactly what makes it interesting. You can learn so much and you kinda have to
learn almost everything at least a little bit to firstly, fly, and secondly,
responsibly fly. You know, these drones are actually very dangerous. They can
easily cut through the skin and make some nasty wounds if you aren't careful. So
after figuring out the "technical" side of the hobby, you also have to know
about your local laws and regulation regarding different fly-zones, distances
you are required to keep from other people etc. This is not made easier by
outdated information, misinformation in forums as well as regulation changes
made just a few years ago. Also, in my case, I not only have to learn about
german regulation, but also EASA, aka EU legislation. I live near the border to
the netherlands, so if I, for example, want to take my drone for a little trip
to the beautiful beaches in the netherlands, I have to also be informed about
those. Most of the german regulation is taken directly from EASA, so if I follow
them I am good to go, but they are very restrictive. In Germany there are
exceptions in regards to model making as a hobby, which makes some things
easier. But those exceptions are only valid in germany, so for the netherlands i
would have to follow the EASA anyways. Yeah, it is a lot. I know probably next
to nothing but I think this gets easier once I start flying. Once you have your
first setup together you can relax on the technology side of things and figure
out where you are allowed to fly and get in the air.

Along side my own research I also joined a discord server in the german FPV
community and engaged in some conversations. I opened a thread where I presented
my shopping list and asked for feedback, where I could get stuff cheaper and if
I am missing something. They are incredibly friendly and open to new people and
quickly helped me out. Most of the stuff I could buy at the manufacturers shop
directly, which safes me quite a bit of money, but I have to wait longer for it
to arrive. A small price to pay, if it saves me up to 100 Euros on a 300-400
Euro purchase. They also told me I should get different kind of batteries.
Generally my list was okay for a beginner though. They also have different parts
in the server to meet up other pilots in your local area so I joined there as
well and told them that until I can fly them myself, I would still be happy to
join other people flying and just watch and learn from them, connect and maybe
try out some things. I can only recommend to join any community for stuff you
are interested in. More often than not you will have some positive experience
and learn a lot faster and easier than by yourself.

## Starting Equipment

So, the shopping list. My first equipment that I will buy is the remote
controller and it's batteries. For that I chose the [Radiomaster Pocket Crush](https://radiomasterrc.com/products/pocket-crush-radio-controller).
It has ELRS builtin, which is the defacto standard for most modern drones. It
also comes in different colors with a solid frame, in comparison to the standard
[Radiomaster Pocket](https://radiomasterrc.com/products/pocket-radio-controller-m2)
which comes only in two different transparent colors. The standard Pocket also
has the option for ELRS but they cost the same as the Crush version and I like
the color options more than the transparent ones. This comes at a price point of
around 70-80 Euro, depending on the store you buy it from. A lot of parts in
this hobby you can get a lot cheaper at asian stores like aliexpress or the
manufacturers themselves. Including the shipping cost, the radio is not one of
them, so I will use a european store to safe in shipping and also have it arrive
in a few days instead of weaks. This radio also needs two 18650 batteries which
I have to buy separately. Those are thankfully a basic standard so I can get
cheap ones for like 4 Euro a piece. The radio is first on the list, as it is
basically the only thing that is also useful without the other things. I can
plug it into the computer and then use if for the simulator. All the other
things are practically useless if I buy them separately. This means I have to
save up a little longer but then I have everything with a single purchase.

All the other parts (except the charger, but I'll get to that) I will buy from
the manufacturer directly. They are all parts from [BetaFPV](https://betafpv.com/)
which means I only have to pay for shipping once and I get them cheaper than
when I buy them from european markets. My shopping cart consists of:

- VR04 analog goggles
- Air75 II drone in the freestyle variant
- Lava II 1S batteries
- and replacement parts for the frame, propellers and camera canopy

I got recommended to buy spare parts as well, since the drone should be very
durable, but stuff can always break and those are all plastic parts which are
very cheap. Having them in stock to replace immediately is better than having to
wait for 3 weeks with a broken drone.

This whole set comes in at around 200 Euro, including shipping costs. Buying
them at european or even german stores, this would be at least 80 Euro more.

Last but not least, I need a charger for the drone batteries. For that,
everybody recommends the [Vifly WhoopStor 3](https://viflydrone.com/collections/lipro-battery-charger/products/vifly-whoopstor-6-ports-1s-battery-storage-charger-discharger).
I will buy this again from the european store since the savings are outweight by
the shipping costs. This comes in at around 35 Euro.

Anybody who can do simple addition knows that this totals to about 325 Euro for
everything, give or take a few euros depending on store and shipping cost. This
is a hefty price and definitely a hurdle for someone to start with this hobby
but this is about as cheap as it can get for useful parts. There are so called
Ready-To-Fly kits (RTF for short), but if you put in a little bit of research,
the parts in those are generally awfully cheap and need to be replaced as soon
as you upgrade. Most often they even break before that. When you buy the parts
yourself you can choose stuff that outlives each other. For example the radio
controller is useful pretty much until eternity, as it supports the industry
standard, has a module bay to extend its functionality in the case of a new
technology emerging and is considered extremely durable. I will probably use it
for a very long time. Next up the goggles. Analog goggles work with every analog
drone camera. So as long as my next drones also transmit the video signal via
analog, I can keep using these goggles. They don't offer a lot of extra features
though, but they do allow me to change the antenna if I notice the range to be
not enough. The drone itself is also considered to be one of the best in it's
price range. It is durable, powerful and incredibly light. This should make it a
very fun thing to fly. The prop guard, small size and light weight make it a
great option to fly indoors as well, so even on bad weather I can practice my
skills by flying in my appartement. If I upgrade to a bigger drone in the future
this will still be a useful backup strategy if I can't get to a spot to fly the
bigger drones. All this is to say, don't buy the absolute cheapest you can find.
You do need to invest a little bit more money into the hobby to have fun and
stay with it.

## Save up money and practice

Is I already stated in the beginning, I just paid 20 bucks for the simulator and
nothing else. After about a week, I already have 16 hours of playtime in it and
I feel the progress. The PS5 controller is annoying, but I have to wait a little
bit until I order the radio. Until then, this is sort of the pre-trial. If it is
still fun in 2 weeks, I buy the radio. After that it is the real trial. I play
on the simulator as long as it is fun, or until I saved up enough money to
afford the rest. If I still like then, I will buy the drone, goggles and rest of
the equipment. I have a tendency of starting things I find fun and after 3-4
weeks never touch it again. This way I can at least ensure myself that the
investment is long lived and worth it.
