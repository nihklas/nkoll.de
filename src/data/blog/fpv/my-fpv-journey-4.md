# Progress

It has been around one and a half months since the last entry in this series.
Since then I flew mostly stuff I can already do and don't really struggle with,
like simple orbits around objects or people, big Split-S around objects and
small rolls or flips. In the last few days I started getting more confident and
tried my hands at more interesting flights. Tried a few Powerloops and dives but
mostly tried to find some interesting spots around my home to fly at and hit
some gaps. There is not much around here but enough to keep going out to fly if
the weather allows it.

At the beginning I met a few times with a friend who has a DJI Neo 2 with the
Goggles N3 and the Motion controller. We sat on an empty soccer field and just
flew around, exploring the (boring) open field spot we were at and tried out
some of the drones capabilities. We found out, that the Air75 is WAY faster than
the Neo 2 for example. They crash the same though. He flew it in a bush of
nettle and while he retrieved it, I also flew my drone into the same bush but
further in. Rescue mission took a long stick to fish it out and a few stings of
the nettle on my legs. Worth it.

# Flying Analog

Flying outside made me realize that the analog video system does have a problem
in seeing branches early enough (or seeing them at all), or longer grass or
other little plants and flowers sticking out of the ground. Flying low over
wildly grown grass is pretty difficult because you could very well just crash
without knowing what you hit. They literally appear out of nowhere, same thing
with trees and small branches sticking out slightly different than the rest of
the tree.

On the other hand the analog recordings have a specific vibe I absolutely love.
I flew in a match of badminton of some friends and the vibe really hit that
"summer evening with friends" and that retro "the good old times" feeling. It's
hard to describe but those videos of friend groups going on adventures and
recording using a VHS Recorder or something of the sorts is what I'm referring
to. If you know what I mean you get it. If not, just watch the videos of analog
FPV flying and compare them to old VHS recordings.

# Some findings about the drone

Now that I am flying the Air75 for some time, I can talk about some things that
you may not think about in the absolute beginning. For example flying in low
light environments. Flying at sunset is quite fun and definitely pretty but
if you face the sun, you cannot see anything besides the sun itself. You can see
maybe a few silhouettes of some trees or buildings at the horizon but everything
else is just black. If you face away from the sun, you have a pretty normal view
though so depending on your spot and what path you are flying that is totally
possible. After the sun has set it gets too dark very quickly. Flying in the
dark is really not possible, but the camera is not made for that and I think
most FPV cameras not specifically made for low light flying struggle at that. I
did try flying a bit at night in a garden of a friend and we had some lights in
the garden so flying up and away from us was just black and I couldn't see
anything, turning around I only saw our garden and with the string lights it
almost looked like a runway for a plane or something. It was quite fun but also
scary because I knew there are trees pretty close and I could not see them.

The drone being that light is also something you have to get used to, especially
coming from only simulator experience. In the simulator you usually fly bigger
drones and the physics are generally not 100% realistic. In both Liftoff and
Uncrashed the drones feel very floaty, which causes some moves to really throw
the drone around and fly quite a but further even with throttle down. The small,
light tinywhoop weighing just about 20 grams without a battery flies totally
different. It is so much more tipsy and immediate to what you tell it to do than
I was used to from the sim. Going of the throttle also makes it immediately fall
down, it doesn't hold it's momentum in any way noticable so you can't sling it
around and let it float like the 5" I was flying in Uncrashed.

Also Wind. The Air75 is supposed to be sturdier in wind than it's smaller
brother, the Air65, but it is still just a tiny drone. It will get blown away in
a strong gust. If you don't plan on flying in a storm, a slight summer breeze
won't destroy your flying but you have to be wary of the fact that going off the
throttle while diving is a moment where the wind will push you around.

To go over more positive findings, the drone is incredible value for money. It
was less than 100 Euro when ordering from BetaFPV directly and even on european
stores it is still no more than 115 Euro. For that amount of money, you get a
tiny drone that is capable of going at least a few hundred meters in an open
field, gives you enough speed to impress people and it's basically
indestructable. The amounts I have crashed this thing is uncountable and the
only thing that actually broke from a crash was a propeller. It was already
quite beaten up and I bent it into place a few times until I finally had a bad
crash against my office chair and one blade snapped of completely. In the
package of the drone you get an extra set of props so I just replaced it and it
flew again without problems.

# I ordered more stuff

I have two cats at home. Flying indoors means I will pick up cat hair. This is
not a big problem, it is usually easy to clean from the motors and props. But it
is annoying to do on every pack swap. So when I ordered the additional
batteries I wanted anyways, I threw in a prop tool to more easily pop off a
prop and clean it without destroying the prop or the frame in the process. Now,
whenever I fly in my home, I can just pop off all the props, take out the motors
and completely get rid of any hair or dust or whatever gets picked up in the
meantime.

I already told about the new batteries. I got 8 more, so I now have 12 in total.
They are all the same 580mAh Lava 2 batteries I got with the drone in the first
place. I don't have a reason to get anything else, since they work as they
should and are suppose to be one of the better ones on the market. I already got
one battery unusable though, somehow the connector on the battery got bent and
now it won't connect to the drone anymore. Putting it in the charger works so I
put it into storage charge and just don't use it. They aren't particularly
expensive and I still have 11 more. With all those batteries I can get basically
as much flight time as I want as I haven't yet had a situation where I used all
packs and wanted to fly more. More often than not I stop without having even
used all the packs because I decided to get to the place to late and it got dark
or I didn't have enough time in the first place. All in all, what I'm trying to
say is, 11 batteries is enough and I won't be needing anymore anytime soon.

One fateful evening I decided that I want to clean a motor. It didn't spin
correctly and it didn't fix itself by making it spin by hand or repeatedly
arming the drone. So I followed a video that showed my how to take apart the
motor itself and get to clean the inside of it. I had to take of a small clamp
on the bottom of the motors. And with small I mean _really_ small. The tutorial
very clearly stated to do it in a bag of some sort not not loose the clamp. I
couldn't get it off so I became frustrated and tried it without the bag. It came
how it had to and I lost the clamp. So that evening I ordered replacement motors
because I couldn't really fly that motor anymore. I probably could, and maybe I
will try to at some point, but I didn't want to risk the rest of the drone with
the motor just disassembled itself midflight. The correct motors only come in a
4-pack so I replaced one and still got 3 more spares. I guess thats good?

Last thing I got was a new antenna for my goggles. The stock antenna on the
VR04s are fine but not great and especially bad with obstacles in the way.
Flying behind a tree immediatly disrupts the signal and makes flying around it
very hard. On discord someone with almost the same setup recommended a specific
antenna (TrueRC Singularity) so I got that one.

One thing you might notice is that the new antenna has worse performance than
the stock antenna on the VR04s. That is because of something called
`polarization`. Please don't ask me what that means, I understand it as a sort
of direction that the antenna speaks in. If you need more information please do
some research, maybe you can enlighten me as well. Anyways, the stock antenna on
the Air75 and on the VR04 are linear polarized. The new antenna is a
_RHCP_-antenna, or _right-hand-circular-polarized_. In my previously stated
understanding, this means the new antenna on the goggles listen in a different
direction than the antenna on the drone is sending. This mismatch is the reason
for a loss in link quality.

# Flight experience

I would like to think I got signifcantly better but I can't really tell. I still
struggle with a lot of basic freestyle moves like landing simple rolls and flips
without corrections. Powerloops are complete hit or miss, sometimes they look
great, sometimes I just fly straight up and do a flip, sometimes I just plant
into the ground. Also, hitting gaps is so much more difficult than I thought,
especially outdoors with a slight bit of wind. You have to really line yourself
up with the gap and then react to a gust blowing you slightly to the right just
before the gap. Most of the flying was very chill, with the occasional trick or
gap in between, some faster cruising around and testing out what the drone can
do. Flying high up and see how far you can go, dropping back down and then
ripping it around a bit. No matter how I fly, it is always very fun. The only
thing I struggle with is going out by myself to fly. I would like to have
someone in the area I can meet up with regularly and go out to fly FPV together
but as of now, I don't really know anyone. I started going out alone and found
it to be fun enough to keep doing it. I just have to get more comfortable going
out and basically sitting by myself. Strangers coming up and talking to me is
something I am both scared of and looking forward to. I would love to have some
people come up, be curious about the drone, ask the same questions of "How fast,
how high, how far" etc. and be amazed by what this tiny little drone is capable
of. On the other hand, I am a bit scared that people see this and immediately
think that I am spying on people, or worse, think this has something to do with
war drones. Either way, I have to get more comfortable with talking to strangers
anyways so I am mostly looking forward to those situations.

# PSA: Don't let your friends who aren't pilots themselves fly your drone

I learned this the hard way. In the beginning I told anyone to go ahead and try
flying the drone. I did put a throttle limit when we were indoors and most
importantly told the to disarm as soon as something happens. For a while, it
worked quite good. People who tried it were either careful enough so that
nothing happened or too scared to push it further than just getting it in the
air and immediately back down. Then I let one person fly, outside on a big
property where I though nothing could happen. Well, they flew very high, lost
orientation, didn't press disarm and tried to see. Finally they disarmed and the
drone fell onto a neighbors roof into the rain gutter. I took over the goggles
and remote, flip the drone around using turtle mode and it fell one roof lower
into the next gutter. This time it was the right side up and I thankfully could
fly out of it no problem. But I didn't learn my lesson immediately so on a
different day, I let another friend fly it on a big soccer field. They again
flew higher than they wanted, drifted backwards and lost orienation. They
disarmed earlier though so the drone didn't move that far. I feared it fell into
a big field of very high grass and we would have to really search for the drone.
To figure out where it was, I used the beeper, but it was to quiet so I armed it
to see and hear if I can make out where it was. Turns out the drone got stuck
way up in a tree right behind us and on arming it fell right down. It had a hard
impact on concrete, right on the battery but nothing happened and the drone
flies normally. The battery also didn't break and flies and charges normally as
well. Both of those situations were way luckier that they had to be and now I
will not let anyone who isn't flying themselves fly the drone. Maybe if I get
the Neo 2 or Avata 2 or a similar drone I can give it to them because they
basically fly themself but I am not sure yet. Also I don't own any of them so
this isn't even a discussion I need to have. Anyways, these drones are way
harder to control than they look like once you have a bit of practice. Everybody
is overwhelmed by them and can't control them on first try. If you aren't ready
to loose or break your drone, don't give them to someone else.

# Next Steps

From now on I will focus more on freestyle moves and finding some nice spots
around my home. I want to eventually get good enough at freestyle to maybe have
some interesting lines to post on social media. Also just the fun of ripping the
drone around obstacles making moves is so much more than simply cruising around
a field. I will continue to do the cruising for some recordings but for flying
fun, freestyling around stuff is so much better. It's also more rewarding,
knowing you just hit a sick line on the spot, going back to the recording and
seeing that it indeed was a sick line is a great feeling. Freestyle is what got
me into this so I will try to get good at it.

As for upgrading my setup, I think I will first save up for some proper goggles.
The VR04s are great goggles for the price and I will recommend them to anyone
starting out on a budget, but they are the most annoying part of the setup as
well. My target is the HDZero Goggles 2, but they cost like 800 bucks so it's
gonna take a while. In the mean time I will probably not need to upgrade
anything else, instead buying mostly spares and repair parts, like new props,
frames, batteries and what else can be considered consumable/replacable. If
anyone has some used HDZero Goggles 2 they want to get rid, hit me up and we can
maybe talk about price.

I am very happy with the experience I have and the progress I made. I want to
keep going and want to get better at freestyle. Eventually upgrading the
goggles and after that maybe targeting a 3" setup to stay below 250g but still
having a lot more power and weight to fling around. Or I just go all the way to
5" immediately and give bando bashing a try. I am not sure yet and it's a long
way to go anyways. We'll see where the road takes us I guess.
